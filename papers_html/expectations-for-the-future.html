在前沿工作塑造了对未来的预期

Working at the frontier shapes expectations for the future · Oct 16, 2026 · Economic Research · 查看原文

📖 中文摘要

本文是Anthropic两份报告的总览，对比在前沿使用AI的员工与公众对未来预期的落差。内部到2026年3月智能体工作小时数已超员工，8月智能体数量达员工六倍，初级与技术岗招聘占比下降，人的注意力成为瓶颈。作者调查员工，并与Morning Consult于8月实施、加权代表成年人口的近11,000名美国成年人调查对比。两群体对AI如何影响劳动力市场持有相似经济模型，预期却差距巨大：39%的美国成年人与91%的员工预期到2030年AI能做出诺贝尔级别发现；超过一半员工预期AI届时能完成其当前工作至少80%，仅5%的美国人如此预期。作者认为落差源自对AI能力的不同亲身体验而非不同理论。

View more We’re sharing two reports: the first covers how AI is changing employment inside Anthropic, and the second**** looks at the gap between how the public and Anthropic employees expect AI to affect work and the economy. Anthropic staff and US workers don’t seem to have different theories about how AI affects jobs. Instead, they have different experiences of what AI capabilities are. Those different experiences seem closely related to perceptions about what will happen to work in the future. Anthropic is a company at the frontier of AI adoption, and that adoption has changed the way we hire. We find that over the past year, we're hiring for a smaller share of junior positions, and a smaller share of our employees work in technical roles. At the same time, the rate of hours worked by AI agents has shot up. By March 2026, agents worked more hours than employees. By August, there were six times more agents than employees at Anthropic, and productivity increased. That’s led to Anthropic producing more code per person, but that’s a notoriously imperfect measure. We see signs of increased productivity elsewhere: we’ve closed more sales per sales representative, and processed more contracts. When our researchers follow the same employee over time, they find the employee’s output rises as their adoption of AI tools increases. All this acceleration means that, increasingly, the bottleneck is human attention. As a result, employees spend more time building ways to better monitor work their agents are doing. Anthropic could be a leading indicator of how AI will shift work organization, hiring, and labor demand across the economy. An AI lab is different from more traditional businesses in how it adopts AI into its work, but research findings about our own workplace may still be instructive about broader transformations, including dynamics for future AI-native companies. Because Anthropic employees experience AI’s effect on work so differently, it stands to reason that they might have different predictions about AI’s effect on the future of work. That’s what our second report investigates. We surveyed Anthropic employees, and compared them to an August survey of almost 11,000 US adults, fielded by Morning Consult and weighted to be representative of the adult population. The public expects AI capabilities to advance rapidly in the coming years; Anthropic staff tend to expect even faster progress. There are a number of questions with big gaps between staff and the public. For example, 39% of US adults—and 91% of staff—expect AI will be able to make a Nobel-level discovery by 2030. More than half of Anthropic staff expect AI to be able to do at least 80% of their current work by 2030; only 5% of Americans expect AI to be that capable. That said, there’s considerable variation in both groups: two out of 10 Anthropic staff have expectations similar to those of the typical US adult. And Anthropic staff and the public also agree on some key questions. In each bucket, people who expect AI to do comparably more of their own work anticipate similar increases in unemployment. In other words, both the public and Anthropic staff have similar economic models of how AI affects the labor market, but ultimately their personal experiences with AI lead them to different expectations for the economy of the future.
