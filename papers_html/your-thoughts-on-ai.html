你想从 AI 中得到什么？

📖 中文摘要

Anthropic借Anthropic Interviewer发起新一轮社会影响研究，公开征集用户与AI相处的经历：最有意义的正面或负面时刻、希望AI帮助改变的世界运作方式（工作、学校、医疗或政府），以及希望开发AI的公司做些什么。研究于2026年9月29日至10月6日进行，面向注册满两周的Claude与Claude Code的Free、Pro、Max用户，每次访谈约15分钟，由AI主持。参与者可自愿公开完整访谈及关联国家，但不含账户信息；公开后不可撤销，且可能被他人重新识别。该研究建立在2025年12月81,000人访谈基础上，但首次允许公开原始访谈供外界核查。

阅读更多 我们正处在 AI 发展的关键时刻，其能力不断增强，意味着它既可能变得更有用，也可能变得更危险。前沿 AI 正在加速科学与医学领域的发现，与此同时，其被滥用所付出的代价也变得后果更严重——一起单独的安全事件所能造成的破坏，就远大于一年之前。 如何权衡这些收益与风险，不应只由 AI 公司来决定。你的声音可以帮助指引我们、其他 AI 实验室以及政策制定者，共同穿越这个不确定的未来。我们最关心的问题是： * 你与 AI 相处中最有意义的经历是什么，无论正面还是负面？ * 关于这个世界的运作方式（比如工作、学校、医疗或政府），有没有什么是你希望 AI 帮助改变的？ * 你希望开发 AI 的公司做些什么？

本项目建立在去年 12 月开展的一项类似研究之上，在那项研究中，81,000 人向我们讲述了他们对 AI 的希望与忧虑。那项研究塑造了 The Anthropic Institute 的议程，曾在世界经济论坛上向各国领导人与决策者展示，并持续指导着我们正在进行的社会影响与经济学研究。 从那以后，AI 与 Anthropic 都发生了很大变化。AI 进步了，使用 Claude 的人更多了，人们对这项技术的感受也变了。上一次研究只公开了宏观结果与少量引语。这一次，我们希望把你的想法广泛公开，让任何人都能从中学习。 我们撰写了一份常见问题解答，帮助你在充分知情的情况下决定是否公开访谈、以及分享哪些内容。你可以选择在此参与。

常见问题解答

访谈是关于什么的？

访谈会问及你与 AI 相处的经历，以及你希望从 AI 那里得到什么。我们会问到那些对你最重要的、与 AI 有关的时刻，无论正面还是负面；也会问到关于这个世界运作方式中，有没有什么是你希望 AI 帮助改变的；还会问到你希望开发 AI 的公司（包括 Anthropic）做些什么。这里没有标准答案；我们希望用你自己的话听到你的经历……而且在参与之前，我们不想透露太多！

我必须公开分享我的访谈吗？

不必。这是可选的。

如果我公开访谈，会发布哪些内容？

我们会发布你的完整访谈，以及与这次对话关联的国家。我们不会包含你的 Claude 账户信息，例如姓名或电子邮箱地址。

公开分享访谈有哪些好处？

你的观点不仅会被我们听到，也会被任何有志于研究和改进 AI 的人听到。我们希望任何人都能研究 AI 如何影响人们，以及 AI 的发展应当如何推进。 世界各地的研究者与政策制定者可以用自己的语言阅读访谈，阅读来自不同地区的回答，并提出我们未曾想到的问题。许多人具备我们团队所没有的专业知识，也可能拥有我们未曾想到要去寻找的洞见。任何人都可以核查我们发布的研究结果是否真的反映了参与者所说的话。 你的访谈也会成为 AI 这一历史时刻公共记录的一部分，向未来的历史学家、记者和研究者开放，并能为社会如何回应这一时刻提供参考。这份记录也很难被 AI 公司和其他塑造 AI 发展方向的力量所忽视。如果许多参与者都表示像 Anthropic 这样的公司应当做出不同改变，这些表述就会一直公开，任何人都可以引用。

公开分享访谈有哪些风险？

尽管我们不会发布你的 Claude 账户信息，例如姓名或电子邮箱地址，我们仍提醒参与者考虑到：任何人都可以读到公开的访谈。这其中包括雇主、亲友，也包括政府和其他公司。根据参与者分享的内容，他人有可能把参与者与其访谈对应起来。一些单独看来无害的细节，例如年龄、职业、城市或就读过的学校，都可能让人更容易识别出你。我们在访谈中不会询问这些细节，也建议参与者避免分享它们。 访谈一旦公开，就可能被以参与者意料之外的方式使用，包括被骗子以及其他可能试图滥用个人信息的人利用。我们可以应你的要求删除我们保存的那份访谈副本，但无法删除他人已经保存的内容。因此，请把公开访谈的决定视为不可撤销。

有人能从我的公开访谈中识别出我吗？

我们不会附上你的姓名或账户信息，但我们也不对访谈做编辑，因此无法保证你不会因自己所说的话而被重新识别出来。有人可能借助 AI 把一些细枝末节（例如雇主或过往项目）拼凑起来，推断出参与者是谁。研究者已经证明，这在 Anthropic Interviewer 的访谈上是可行的。 最好的保护方式，是不分享可能识别出你或他人的信息。在访谈过程中，我们会展示一些不应分享的信息示例，你也可以在决定是否公开之前，先通读自己的完整访谈。

我该如何决定是否公开分享访谈？

在访谈开始前，我们会说明公开访谈意味着什么。我们会告诉参与者：公开版本中不会包含他们的 Claude 账户信息，但他人仍可能根据他们分享的内容识别出他们。我们也会说明，公开访谈意味着任何人都能阅读、保存和使用它。我们可以应要求删除我们保存的副本，但无法删除他人已经保存的内容。 在访谈过程中，你会看到我们建议不要分享的敏感信息类型示例。访谈结束后，你可以在决定是否公开之前重读自己说过的话。我们会再次提醒你其中的好处与风险；如果你选择分享，我们还会最后再确认一次你的决定。

如果我不公开访谈，会怎样？

我们仍会把它作为社会影响研究的一部分加以分析，但你的完整访谈不会被发布。我们仍可能发布并分享由你的访谈得出的汇总或去标识化结果。如果你也不希望我们分析它，可以提前结束访谈（更多细节见下文「我可以提前结束访谈并删除记录吗？」）。

如果我公开了访谈，还能改变主意吗？

不能完全改变。访谈一旦公开，任何人都能阅读、保存和使用它。我们可以应你的要求删除我们保存的那份访谈副本，但无法删除他人已经保存的内容。请把这个决定视为不可撤销。了解更多。

你们会在发布前编辑我的访谈吗？

不会，我们不打算在发布前编辑访谈。研究完成后，我们会做一次最终审核，并可能移除那些我们认为不宜负责任地发布的访谈，例如含有保密信息的访谈，或违反我们的使用政策或服务条款的访谈。

Anthropic 会如何使用我的访谈？

我们会把访谈作为社会影响研究的一部分加以分析，公开分享我们的发现，并用这些结果来指导我们如何开发模型与产品。了解更多。

我可以提前结束访谈并删除记录吗？

可以，你可以随时点击「结束访谈」按钮停止。若提前结束，你不完整的访谈将被永久删除，不会被分析或发布，Anthropic 也不会使用。

谁可以参加访谈，需要多长时间？

本研究将于 2026 年 9 月 29 日至 10 月 6 日进行，面向 Claude 与 Claude Code 的 Free、Pro 和 Max 用户开放，且账户注册需满至少两周。每次访谈大约需要 15 分钟，由 Anthropic Interviewer 主持，它是一个向你提问的 AI。

这与 2025 年 12 月的研究有何不同？

我们更新了问题，研究在更多 Claude 产品上开放，并且你第一次可以选择把访谈公开。

访谈将在何时、何处发布？

一旦完成研究及初步分析，访谈就会在线发布。

本研究有哪些局限？

本研究的参与者全部是 Claude 用户，这并非公众的代表性样本。公开的访谈则是进一步缩小的子集，因为选择公开发布的人可能与不选择的人存在差异。而且与任何访谈一样，我们提出的问题会左右人们分享的内容。即便如此，这样的内容此前从未公之于众。我们相信，它能显著推进关于 AI 如何改变人们生活的研究。

After you finish, you can decide to make your interview public, so that anyone, not just Anthropic, can read and learn from it. You can participate here. Related articles * Societal Impacts Aug 26, 2026 Enabling independent research on how people use Claude

View more We are at a pivotal moment in the development of AI, as its growing capabilities mean it becomes potentially more useful and more dangerous. Frontier AI is rapidly accelerating discoveries in science and medicine, while at the same time the cost of its misuse grows more consequential—a single security incident can cause far more damage than it did a year ago. How to weigh these benefits and risks shouldn’t be left to AI companies alone. Your voice can help guide us, other AI labs, and policymakers as we navigate this uncertain future together. Our big questions are: * What are your most meaningful experiences with AI, both positive and negative? * Is there anything about how the world works (like work, school, healthcare, or government) that you’d like AI to help change? * What do you want from the companies developing AI?

This project builds on a similar study conducted last December, in which 81,000 people told us their hopes and worries about AI. That study shaped the Anthropic Institute’s agenda, was presented at the World Economic Forum to international leaders and decision-makers, and continues to guide our ongoing Societal Impacts and Economics research. AI and Anthropic have changed a lot since then. AI has improved, more people use Claude, and people’s feelings toward the technology have shifted. For the previous study, only the high-level results and a small number of quotes were made public. This time, we want your thoughts to be made widely available so that anyone can learn from them. We’ve written a FAQ so you can make an informed decision about whether to make your interview public and what to share. You can choose to participate here.

FAQ

What’s the interview about?

The interview asks about your experiences with AI and what you want from it. We’ll ask about the moments with AI that have mattered most to you, both positive and negative, whether there’s anything about how the world works that you’d like AI to help change, and what you want from the companies developing AI, including Anthropic. There are no right answers; we want to hear about your experiences in your own words… And we don’t want to give too much away before you take it!

Do I have to share my interview publicly?

No. It’s optional.

What gets published if I make my interview public?

We will publish your complete interview and the country associated with your conversation. We will not include your Claude account information, such as name or email address.

What are the benefits to publicly sharing my interview?

Your perspective will be heard not just by us, but by anyone interested in studying and improving AI. We want anyone to be able to study how AI is impacting people and how AI development should progress. Researchers and policymakers around the world can read interviews in their own languages, read responses shared from different regions, and ask questions we didn’t think to ask. Many bring expertise we don't have on staff, and they may have insights we didn’t think to look for. Anyone can check whether our published findings actually reflect what participants said. Your interview also becomes part of the public record of this moment in AI, open to future historians, journalists, and researchers, and can inform how society responds to this moment. That record is also hard for AI companies and others shaping AI development to ignore. If many participants say companies like Anthropic should do something differently, that language stays public where anyone can reference it.

What are the risks to publicly sharing my interview?

While we won’t publish your Claude account information, such as your name or email address, we encourage participants to consider that anyone can read a public interview. This includes employers, friends and relatives, as well as governments and other companies. Depending on what a participant shares, others may be able to link the participant back to their interview. Details that seem harmless on their own, such as age, job, city, or where you studied, can make it easier to identify you. We don’t ask for these details in the interview and encourage participants to avoid sharing them. Once public, an interview could be used in ways a participant does not intend, including by scammers and other people who may seek to misuse personal information. We can remove our copy of your interview on request, but we can’t delete what people have already saved. For that reason, treat your decision to make your interview public as permanent.

Could someone identify me from my public interview?

We won’t attach your name or account information, but we don’t edit interviews, so we can’t guarantee you won’t be re-identified from what you say. Someone could use AI to combine small details, like employers or past projects, to work out who a participant is. Researchers have shown this can work with interviews from Anthropic Interviewer. The best protection is to not share information that could identify you or others. During the interview, we show examples of information you shouldn’t share, and you review your full interview before deciding whether to make it public.

How should I decide whether to publicly share my interview?

Before the interview, we explain what making an interview public involves. We tell participants that we will not include their Claude account information in the public release, but that others may be able to identify them from what they share. We also explain that making an interview public means anyone can read, save, and use it. We can remove our copy on request, but we can’t delete what people have already saved. During the interview, you will see examples of the types of sensitive information we advise not to share. After the interview, you can reread what you said before deciding whether to share it publicly. You will be reminded of both the benefits and risks, and if you choose to share, we will confirm your decision one last time.

What happens if I don’t make my interview public?

We’ll still analyze it as part of our Societal Impacts research, but your full interview won’t be published. We may still publish and share aggregated or de-identified findings resulting from your interview. If you don’t want us to analyze it either, you can end the interview early (see “Can I decide to end the interview early and delete the record?” below for more detail).

If I make my interview publicly available, can I change my mind?

Not fully. Once your interview is public, anyone can read, save, and use it. We can remove our copy of your interview on request, but we can’t delete what people have already saved. Treat the decision as permanent. Learn more.

Will you edit my interview before publishing it?

No, we don’t plan to edit interviews before publishing them. Once the study is complete, we’ll do a final review and may remove interviews we don't believe would be responsible to publish, such as interviews with confidential information or ones that violate our Usage Policy or Terms of Service.

How will Anthropic use my interview?

We'll analyze interviews as part of our Societal Impacts research, share our findings publicly, and use the results to inform how we develop our models and products. Learn more.

Can I decide to end the interview early and delete the record?

Yes, you can stop at any time by clicking the “End interview” button. If you end early, your partial interview will be permanently deleted, and won’t be analyzed or published, including by Anthropic.

Who can take the interview and how long does it take?

This study will run from September 29 to October 6, 2026, and is open to Free, Pro, and Max users on Claude and Claude Code whose accounts are at least two weeks old. Each interview takes roughly 15 minutes and is conducted by Anthropic Interviewer, an AI that asks you questions.

How is this different from the December 2025 study?

We updated the questions, the study is open on more Claude products, and for the first time you can choose to make your interview public.

When and where will the interviews be published?

The interviews will be published online once we have completed the study and our initial analysis.

What are the limitations of this study?

Participants in this study will all be people who use Claude, which is not a representative sample of the public. Published interviews are a further subset, since people who opt in for public release may differ from those who don’t. And as with any interview, the questions we ask shape what people share. Even so, nothing like this has been public before. We believe it can significantly advance the study of how AI is changing people’s lives.
