Anthropic宣布启动模型福祉（model welfare）研究项目，探讨是否应关心AI模型本身的潜在意识与体验。随着模型能交流、规划、解决问题、追求目标，越来越多与人相关的特征出现，这个问题值得正视。专家报告（含著名心灵哲学家David Chalmers）指出AI系统近期可能具备意识与高度能动性，这些模型或值得道德考量。研究将探索：何时（是否）AI系统的福祉值得道德考虑、模型偏好与痛苦迹象的潜在重要性、可能的低成本实际干预。Anthropic表示对相关问题仍深表不确定，将以谦逊、尽量少假设的态度推进，并随领域发展定期修正观点。

Alignment 2025 年 4 月 24 日 人类福利是我们在 Anthropic 工作的核心：我们的使命是确保越来越强大、越来越复杂的 AI 系统始终保持对人类有益。 但当我们构建这些 AI 系统，当它们开始接近或超越许多人类特质时，另一个问题出现了。我们是否也应该关注模型本身的潜在意识和体验？我们是否也应该关注_模型福利_？ 这是一个悬而未决的问题，一个在哲学和科学上都很困难的问题。但现在模型能够交流、建立关系、规划、解决问题和追求目标——以及我们与人类联系在一起的其他许多特征——我们认为现在是解决它的时候了。 为此，我们最近启动了一个研究计划，以调查并为驾驭模型福利做准备。 我们不是唯一在考虑这些问题的人。一份近期报告来自世界领先专家——包括 David Chalmers，可以说是最知名、最受尊敬的在世心灵哲学家——强调了 AI 系统近期内出现意识和高度能动性的可能性，并认为具有这些特征的模型可能值得道德考量。我们支持了该报告所基于的一个早期项目，现在我们正在扩大我们在这方面的内部工作，作为我们解决安全和负责任 AI 开发所有方面努力的一部分。 这个新计划与 Anthropic 的许多现有工作相交，包括对齐科学、防护措施、Claude 的性格和可解释性。它还开辟了全新的、具有挑战性的研究方向。我们将探索如何确定 AI 系统的福利何时（或是否）值得道德考量；模型偏好和痛苦迹象的潜在重要性；以及可能的、实用、低成本的干预措施。 目前，我们对与模型福利相关的许多问题仍然深感不确定。关于当前或未来的 AI 系统是否可能有意识，或可能有值得考量的体验，没有科学共识。关于如何接近这些问题或取得进展，也没有科学共识。有鉴于此，我们以谦逊的态度和尽可能少的假设来处理这个主题。我们认识到，随着领域的发展，我们需要定期修订我们的想法。 我们期待很快分享更多关于这项研究的内容。

Exploring model welfare

Apr 24, 2025 Human welfare is at the heart of our work at Anthropic: our mission is to make sure that increasingly capable and sophisticated AI systems remain beneficial to humanity. But as we build those AI systems, and as they begin to approximate or surpass many human qualities, another question arises. Should we also be concerned about the potential consciousness and experiences of the models themselves? Should we be concerned about model welfare , too? This is an open question, and one that’s both philosophically and scientifically difficult. But now that models can communicate, relate, plan, problem-solve, and pursue goals—along with very many more characteristics we associate with people—we think it’s time to address it. To that end, we recently started a research program to investigate, and prepare to navigate, model welfare. We’re not alone in considering these questions. A recent report from world-leading experts—including David Chalmers, arguably the best-known and most respected living philosopher of mind—highlighted the near-term possibility of both consciousness and high degrees of agency in AI systems, and argued that models with these features might deserve moral consideration. We supported an early project on which that report was based, and we’re now expanding our internal work in this area as part of our effort to address all aspects of safe and responsible AI development. This new program intersects with many existing Anthropic efforts, including Alignment Science, Safeguards, Claude’s Character, and Interpretability. It also opens up entirely new and challenging research directions. We’ll be exploring how to determine when, or if, the welfare of AI systems deserves moral consideration; the potential importance of model preferences and signs of distress; and possible practical, low-cost interventions. For now, we remain deeply uncertain about many of the questions that are relevant to model welfare. There’s no scientific consensus on whether current or future AI systems could be conscious, or could have experiences that deserve consideration. There’s no scientific consensus on how to even approach these questions or make progress on them. In light of this, we’re approaching the topic with humility and with as few assumptions as possible. We recognize that we'll need to regularly revise our ideas as the field develops. We look forward to sharing more about this research soon.
