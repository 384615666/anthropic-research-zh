职业再培训项目的效果证据综述

Reviewing the evidence on worker retraining programs · Aug 12, 2026 · Economics · 查看原文

📖 中文摘要

Anthropic经济研究团队（David Roodman与Maxim Massenkoff）发布对劳动者再培训项目的证据综述，整合56项美国随机研究与欧洲实验证据进行荟萃分析。结果显示，职业培训项目效果为正但有限：每提供一个培训名额，就业率提升2-3个百分点、年收入增加约1000美元，而成本约1.3万美元；计入新增税收与减少的福利支出后政府可回收过半投入，整体大致收支平衡。少数“行业定向项目”收益高出数倍但难以复制。作者结论：若AI大规模替代劳动力，现有再培训项目很可能力有不逮，建议现在就投资验证并扩展示范项目。

经济（Economics） 2026年8月12日 下载报告 我们在此分享一份关于职业再培训项目证据的综述，由独立研究者 David Roodman 与 Anthropic 的 Maxim Massenkoff 合著。

再培训劳动者，是缓解 AI 带来的劳动力市场冲击最流行的政策选项。在本报告中，作者探讨了在面对重大的劳动力市场冲击时，这些项目是否能真正发挥作用。

本综述是我们经济研究团队关于 AI 对经济影响的工作的一部分。我们的经济指数追踪 AI 在各职业和行业中的使用情况。今年早些时候，我们发布了一个框架，用于衡量 AI 对劳动力市场的影响并识别最有可能受到冲击的岗位。我们的经济政策框架提出了一系列情景下可能的政策应对，其中包括劳动者再培训。我们希望了解每一项此类政策应对背后的证据。

本综述借鉴了 56 项美国随机研究，并将其合并进一项新的荟萃分析，同时结合了来自欧洲的实验证据。平均而言，职业培训项目产生的效果是正向但有限的：每提供一个培训名额，就业率上升两到三个百分点，年收入增加约 1,000 美元，而成本约为 13,000 美元。计入增加的税收和减少的福利支出后，政府能收回其支出的一半以上，项目整体上大致收支平衡。

一小部分“行业项目”（sector programs）——即与高需求行业中的雇主合作、并将受训者直接安置到该行业岗位的项目——产生的收益要大好几倍。但复制这些项目的尝试往往以失败告终。作者的结论是，如果 AI 大规模取代劳动者，现有的再培训项目很可能力有不逮。

他们的核心建议是，现在就投入资源去验证、评估并推广最有前景的项目，包括通过一项努力，快速扩展开设一个面向特定劳动者群体的领先项目，并对其结果进行严格测量。我们的经济未来研究基金正是为资助此类问题的研究而设立的。

Reviewing the evidence on worker retraining programs

Aug 12, 2026 Download the report We're sharing a review of the evidence on worker retraining programs, coauthored by independent researcher David Roodman and Anthropic's Maxim Massenkoff. Retraining workers is the most popular policy option for mitigating labor market disruption from AI. In this report, the authors investigate whether these programs would work in the face of significant labor market disruption. The review is part of our Economic Research team's work on AI's effects on the economy. Our Economic Index tracks how AI is being used across occupations and industries. Earlier this year, we published a framework for measuring AI's effects on the labor market and identifying the jobs most likely to be affected. Our Economic Policy Framework sets out possible policy responses, including worker retraining, across a range of scenarios. We want to understand the evidence behind each of these policy responses. The review draws on 56 randomized US studies, combined in a new meta-analysis, along with experimental evidence from Europe. On average, job training programs produce positive but modest effects: for each person offered a training slot, employment rises by two to three percentage points and earnings by roughly $1,000 a year, against a cost of about $13,000. Counting the added tax revenue and reduced benefit payments, the government recovers more than half of what it spends, and programs roughly break even overall. A small set of “sector programs”—programs that partner with employers in a high-demand industry and place people directly into jobs in that field—produce gains several times larger. But attempts to replicate them have often failed. The authors conclude that if AI displaces workers at scale, existing retraining programs would likely fall short. Their central recommendation is to invest now in demonstrating, evaluating, and scaling the most promising programs, including through an effort to rapidly expand a leading program for a specific group of workers and rigorously measure the results. Our Economic Futures Research Fund is designed to fund investigations into questions like these. Read the report here.
